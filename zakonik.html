THE OFFICIAL BOOK OF LAWS — VOLUME II • §§ 1–288

PREAMBLE

We, the Brothers bound by loyalty, trust, honor, and mutual respect, hereby establish this Book of Laws as the legal foundation of the Bro Code.

Its purpose shall be to preserve the principles of brotherhood, regulate the conduct of its members, establish consequences for violations, and ensure that no Brother shall be abandoned, betrayed, or forgotten without just cause.

The provisions herein shall apply to all recognized Brothers, regardless of seniority, status, popularity, or personal influence.

TITLE I — GENERAL PROVISIONS

§ 1. Purpose of the Code

(1) This Code shall establish the legal framework governing the Brotherhood.

(2) Its provisions shall regulate:

a) the rights and duties of Brothers;

b) the protection of brotherly trust;

c) the resolution of disputes;

d) the classification of violations;

e) the administration of penalties;

f) the recognition and protection of Brotherhood traditions.

(3) No provision shall be interpreted in a manner that contradicts the fundamental principle of mutual respect.

§ 2. Definitions

For the purposes of this Code:

(1) Brother means a recognized member of the Brotherhood.

(2) Brotherhood means the collective body of recognized Brothers.

(3) Council means the governing assembly established under this Code.

(4) Violation means any action or omission contrary to the provisions of this Code.

(5) Brotherly Trust means the mutual confidence upon which the Brotherhood is founded.

(6) Wingman means a Brother formally or informally assisting another Brother in a social situation.

(7) Former Brother means a person whose membership has been suspended or terminated.

(8) Emergency means a situation involving immediate danger, serious distress, or circumstances requiring urgent assistance.

§ 3. Scope of Application

(1) This Code shall apply to all recognized Brothers.

(2) Its provisions shall apply during:

a) private gatherings;

b) public events;

c) group communications;

d) social interactions;

e) journeys and shared activities;

f) any situation in which the conduct of one Brother materially affects another Brother.

(3) A Brother shall not escape responsibility merely because a violation occurred outside the presence of other members.

§ 4. Supremacy of the Code

(1) This Code shall constitute the highest internal authority of the Brotherhood.

(2) All traditions, customs, agreements, and individual privileges shall remain subordinate to its provisions.

(3) In the event of a conflict between a custom and an express provision, the express provision shall prevail.

(4) No individual Brother shall possess the authority to exempt himself from the Code.

§ 5. Fundamental Principles

The Brotherhood shall be governed by the following principles:

a) Brothers Before Others;

b) Loyalty Before Convenience;

c) Trust Before Reputation;

d) Respect Before Authority;

e) Brotherhood Before Personal Advantage.

(2) These principles shall guide the interpretation of every provision herein.

TITLE II — RIGHTS OF BROTHERS

§ 6. Right to Brotherhood

(1) Every recognized Brother shall have the right to be treated with dignity and respect.

(2) No Brother shall be deliberately excluded from the Brotherhood without reasonable cause and due consideration.

(3) Membership shall not depend solely upon popularity, financial status, appearance, or social influence.

§ 7. Right to Loyalty

(1) Every Brother shall be entitled to reasonable loyalty from fellow Brothers.

(2) No Brother shall be required to support conduct that is dishonest, harmful, or contrary to the fundamental principles of this Code.

(3) Loyalty shall not constitute an obligation to conceal serious wrongdoing.

§ 8. Right to Privacy

(1) Every Brother shall have the right to privacy in personal communications and private matters.

(2) No Brother shall disclose confidential information without authorization.

(3) This provision shall not prohibit disclosure reasonably necessary to protect a person from danger or obtain appropriate assistance.

§ 9. Right to Defense

(1) No Brother shall be declared guilty of a violation without an opportunity to present his account of the matter.

(2) Every accused Brother shall have the right to:

a) know the allegations against him;

b) respond to the allegations;

c) present relevant evidence;

d) request a fair hearing before the Council.

§ 10. Right to a Second Chance

(1) A Brother found responsible for a violation may request reconsideration of his membership status.

(2) The Council shall consider the seriousness of the violation, the circumstances, and any subsequent conduct.

(3) No Brother shall possess an unconditional right to forgiveness.

TITLE III — DUTIES AND OBLIGATIONS OF BROTHERS

§ 11. General Duty of Brotherhood

(1) Every Brother shall conduct himself in a manner consistent with the principles of loyalty, honesty, and mutual respect.

(2) A Brother shall not knowingly act in a manner intended to undermine the unity of the Brotherhood.

(3) Every Brother shall make reasonable efforts to preserve the trust placed in him by fellow Brothers.

(4) No Brother shall be required to sacrifice his personal safety, dignity, or fundamental rights in order to fulfill an obligation under this Code.

§ 12. Duty of Loyalty

(1) Every Brother shall act in good faith toward fellow Brothers.

(2) A Brother shall not knowingly deceive another Brother for personal advantage.

(3) A Brother shall not secretly cooperate with another person for the purpose of humiliating, exploiting, or betraying a Brother.

(4) Loyalty shall be measured by conduct rather than declarations.

§ 13. Duty of Honesty

(1) Every Brother shall provide truthful information when such information is reasonably necessary to protect the interests of another Brother.

(2) Deliberate concealment of a material fact may constitute a violation of this Code.

(3) A Brother shall not fabricate evidence, invent accusations, or knowingly spread false information concerning another Brother.

(4) Minor social exaggerations shall not constitute a violation unless used to cause material harm.

§ 14. Duty of Assistance

(1) A Brother shall make reasonable efforts to assist another Brother who is experiencing a genuine emergency.

(2) Assistance may include:

a) providing transportation;

b) offering temporary shelter where appropriate;

c) contacting emergency services;

d) providing emotional support;

e) helping resolve an urgent practical problem.

(3) The inability to provide assistance shall not constitute a violation where the Brother has made reasonable efforts to obtain alternative help.

§ 15. Duty of Confidentiality

(1) Information entrusted to a Brother in confidence shall remain confidential.

(2) A Brother shall not disclose such information to outsiders without permission.

(3) Confidentiality shall survive the termination of membership.

(4) Disclosure shall be permitted where reasonably necessary to protect a person from serious harm.

§ 16. Duty of Respect

(1) Every Brother shall respect the personal boundaries of fellow Brothers.

(2) No Brother shall use humiliation, intimidation, or deliberate embarrassment as a means of asserting authority.

(3) Disagreement shall not excuse disrespectful conduct.

(4) A Brother shall respect the right of another Brother to decline an invitation, challenge, request, or social activity.

§ 17. Duty of Reliability

(1) A Brother who accepts an obligation shall make reasonable efforts to fulfill it.

(2) If fulfillment becomes impossible, the Brother shall notify the affected members as soon as reasonably practicable.

(3) Repeated cancellations without reasonable explanation may constitute a minor violation.

(4) A genuine emergency shall excuse non-fulfillment where appropriate.

§ 18. Duty to Protect Brotherhood Traditions

(1) Every Brother shall respect traditions formally recognized by the Brotherhood.

(2) No Brother shall deliberately sabotage an established tradition for the purpose of humiliating its participants.

(3) Traditions shall remain voluntary unless otherwise agreed by the participating Brothers.

(4) No tradition shall override personal safety, consent, or dignity.

TITLE IV — ROMANTIC CONDUCT AND CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

§ 19. Protection of a Brother's Former Relationship

(1) No Brother shall knowingly pursue a romantic relationship with the former partner of another Brother without first considering the circumstances and discussing the matter with the affected Brother.

(2) The seriousness and duration of the previous relationship shall be taken into account.

(3) The passage of time may be considered but shall not automatically eliminate the obligation of respect.

(4) The former partner shall retain full freedom to make independent decisions.

§ 20. Prohibition of Secret Pursuit

(1) A Brother shall not secretly pursue a person whom another Brother has openly and seriously expressed an interest in, where such conduct is intended to undermine the latter's efforts.

(2) Where two Brothers develop an interest in the same person, they should disclose the conflict honestly.

(3) No Brother shall fabricate information about another Brother to obtain a romantic advantage.

(4) No provision of this section shall grant any Brother ownership or exclusive rights over another person's affection.

§ 21. Romantic Conflicts of Interest

(1) A romantic conflict of interest shall arise where the romantic actions of one Brother may materially affect the trust or relationship between two or more Brothers.

(2) The affected Brothers should attempt to resolve the matter through direct communication.

(3) Deliberate concealment of relevant information may constitute a breach of trust.

(4) The Council may review the matter where a serious violation is alleged.

§ 22. The Wingman Obligation

(1) A Brother formally acting as a Wingman shall make reasonable efforts to assist the designated Brother.

(2) Such assistance may include:

a) introducing individuals;

b) facilitating conversation;

c) providing honest advice;

d) helping the designated Brother leave an uncomfortable situation;

e) offering support following rejection.

(3) A Wingman shall not manipulate, pressure, or deceive any person on behalf of another Brother.

(4) The Wingman obligation shall terminate upon withdrawal of consent by either participating Brother.

§ 23. Prohibition of Romantic Sabotage

(1) No Brother shall deliberately interfere with another Brother's consensual romantic relationship for personal amusement or advantage.

(2) Sabotage may include:

a) knowingly spreading false rumors;

b) impersonating another person;

c) deliberately misrepresenting private conversations;

d) interfering with agreed social arrangements.

(3) The seriousness of the violation shall depend on intent and consequences.

§ 24. Protection Following a Breakup

(1) A Brother experiencing a breakup shall be entitled to reasonable support from fellow Brothers.

(2) Other Brothers shall not disclose private information obtained during the former relationship.

(3) No Brother shall be required to maintain contact with a former partner.

(4) A Brother shall not be pressured into beginning a new relationship before he is ready.

§ 25. The Right to Romantic Independence

(1) Every Brother shall retain the right to make independent romantic decisions.

(2) No Brother shall be required to obtain Council approval before entering a consensual relationship.

(3) The Brotherhood may express concerns where a relationship presents a genuine risk of harm.

(4) Such concerns shall be expressed respectfully and without coercion.

TITLE V — FINANCIAL OBLIGATIONS AND PROPERTY RIGHTS

§ 26. General Financial Responsibility

(1) Every Brother shall remain responsible for his own financial obligations.

(2) Brotherhood shall not create an automatic obligation to provide money, goods, or services.

(3) Any financial arrangement between Brothers shall be based upon mutual understanding.

(4) A Brother shall not exploit the generosity of another Brother.

§ 27. Loans Between Brothers

(1) Any loan exceeding an amount considered insignificant by the parties should be clearly agreed upon.

(2) The parties should establish:

a) the amount borrowed;

b) the repayment date;

c) any agreed conditions;

d) whether the amount constitutes a loan or a gift.

(3) A Brother shall not deliberately avoid repayment.

(4) Where repayment becomes impossible, the borrowing Brother shall communicate the circumstances honestly.

§ 28. Shared Expenses

(1) Brothers participating in a shared activity shall contribute according to the arrangement agreed upon beforehand.

(2) No Brother shall be presumed responsible for expenses he did not agree to assume.

(3) A Brother who pays on behalf of the group may request repayment of the agreed shares.

(4) Disputes concerning shared expenses shall be resolved through direct communication.

§ 29. Protection Against Financial Exploitation

(1) No Brother shall repeatedly obtain financial benefits through deception or emotional pressure.

(2) A Brother shall not falsely represent his financial circumstances to obtain money from another Brother.

(3) Financial assistance shall remain voluntary.

(4) A refusal to lend money shall not constitute disloyalty.

§ 30. Ownership of Personal Property

(1) Every Brother shall retain exclusive ownership of his personal property unless otherwise agreed.

(2) Temporary possession shall not transfer ownership.

(3) No Brother shall sell, lend, damage, or dispose of another Brother's property without permission.

(4) Any damage caused through negligence or deliberate misconduct shall be subject to appropriate restitution.

§ 31. Borrowed Property

(1) A Brother borrowing property shall exercise reasonable care.

(2) The property shall be returned within the agreed period.

(3) The borrower shall notify the owner of any damage or loss.

(4) Failure to return borrowed property without reasonable explanation may constitute a violation.

§ 32. Digital Property and Accounts

(1) Personal accounts, devices, passwords, and private files shall remain protected.

(2) No Brother shall access another Brother's account without permission.

(3) The sharing of passwords shall not constitute permanent authorization for unrestricted access.

(4) Unauthorized publication or alteration of another Brother's digital information may constitute a serious violation.

TITLE VI — THE COUNCIL OF BROTHERS AND JUDICIAL AUTHORITY

§ 33. Establishment of the Council

(1) There is hereby established an institution known as the Council of Brothers, hereinafter referred to as "the Council".

(2) The Council shall serve as the principal governing and judicial body of the Brotherhood.

(3) Its purpose shall be to:

a) interpret the provisions of this Code;

b) resolve disputes between Brothers;

c) examine alleged violations;

d) impose disciplinary measures;

e) approve amendments to this Code;

f) preserve the traditions and principles of the Brotherhood.

§ 34. Composition of the Council

(1) The Council shall consist of recognized Brothers selected by the Brotherhood.

(2) The number of Council members should be odd whenever reasonably possible.

(3) Every Council member shall possess equal voting rights unless otherwise established by an approved constitutional amendment.

(4) No Brother shall automatically receive a permanent Council seat solely by virtue of seniority.

§ 35. Appointment of Council Members

(1) Council members shall be appointed through a process recognized by the Brotherhood.

(2) Appointment shall take into consideration:

a) loyalty;

b) reliability;

c) impartiality;

d) knowledge of the Code;

e) willingness to serve the Brotherhood.

(3) A Brother under active disciplinary investigation may be temporarily excluded from appointment.

(4) The appointment shall be recorded in the official Register of the Council.

§ 36. The Presiding Brother

(1) The Council shall elect one of its members to serve as Presiding Brother.

(2) The Presiding Brother shall:

a) convene Council meetings;

b) maintain order during proceedings;

c) oversee voting procedures;

d) announce official decisions;

e) ensure that every participating Brother receives an opportunity to speak.

(3) The Presiding Brother shall not possess unilateral authority to overturn a Council decision.

§ 37. Jurisdiction of the Council

(1) The Council shall have jurisdiction over disputes involving alleged violations of this Code.

(2) Its jurisdiction shall extend to matters concerning:

a) betrayal of Brotherly Trust;

b) financial misconduct;

c) violations of confidentiality;

d) abuse of Council authority;

e) repeated violations of Brotherhood traditions;

f) disputes concerning membership.

(3) The Council shall not interfere in purely personal matters that do not materially affect the Brotherhood.

§ 38. Impartiality of Council Members

(1) Every Council member shall act impartially.

(2) A Council member personally involved in a dispute shall disclose such involvement.

(3) Such member may be excluded from voting on the matter.

(4) No Council member shall use his position to settle personal grudges.

§ 39. Council Meetings

(1) Ordinary Council meetings may be held whenever necessary.

(2) Extraordinary meetings may be convened upon request of at least one-third of the Council members.

(3) Every meeting shall include an agenda.

(4) Decisions shall be recorded in the official Council Register.

§ 40. Quorum

(1) A Council meeting shall be considered valid when more than half of its active members are present.

(2) No binding disciplinary judgment shall be issued without a valid quorum.

(3) In exceptional circumstances, the Council may postpone a decision until sufficient members are available.

§ 41. Voting Procedure

(1) Each eligible Council member shall possess one vote.

(2) Ordinary decisions shall be adopted by a simple majority.

(3) Serious disciplinary decisions may require a two-thirds majority.

(4) Constitutional amendments may require unanimous approval where expressly provided by this Code.

§ 42. Official Council Records

(1) Every significant Council decision shall be documented.

(2) Records should include:

a) the nature of the dispute;

b) the relevant provisions;

c) the decision reached;

d) the applicable disciplinary measure;

e) the date of the decision.

(3) Confidential information shall not be unnecessarily included in publicly accessible records.

TITLE VII — OFFENCES AND CLASSIFICATION OF VIOLATIONS

§ 43. General Definition of an Offence

(1) An offence shall consist of an action or omission that violates a provision of this Code.

(2) Responsibility shall depend upon the nature of the conduct, the intention of the Brother, and the circumstances of the case.

(3) Accidental conduct shall not automatically be treated as deliberate misconduct.

§ 44. Classification of Offences

(1) Violations shall be divided into four principal categories:

a) Class I — Minor Offences;

b) Class II — Moderate Offences;

c) Class III — Serious Offences;

d) Class IV — Grave Offences.

(2) The classification shall determine the maximum disciplinary measures available.

(3) The Council may consider aggravating and mitigating circumstances.

§ 45. Class I — Minor Offences

(1) Minor offences shall include conduct that causes limited inconvenience or temporary disruption.

(2) Examples may include:

a) repeatedly arriving late without reasonable explanation;

b) failing to communicate a change of plans;

c) neglecting an agreed minor obligation;

d) temporarily disregarding an established tradition.

(3) Such offences shall ordinarily be resolved through a warning or informal correction.

§ 46. Class II — Moderate Offences

(1) Moderate offences shall include repeated or intentional misconduct that undermines the trust between Brothers.

(2) Examples may include:

a) repeatedly failing to fulfill agreed obligations;

b) knowingly excluding a Brother from important group arrangements;

c) refusing to return borrowed property;

d) repeatedly violating reasonable confidentiality expectations.

(3) The Council may impose formal disciplinary measures.

§ 47. Class III — Serious Offences

(1) Serious offences shall include conduct involving deliberate deception, significant betrayal, or substantial damage to the Brotherhood.

(2) Examples may include:

a) knowingly disclosing confidential information;

b) deliberately sabotaging another Brother's important relationship or opportunity;

c) financial exploitation;

d) repeated violations following previous disciplinary decisions;

e) abusing Council authority for personal benefit.

(3) Such offences shall require a formal hearing.

§ 48. Class IV — Grave Offences

(1) Grave offences shall constitute the most serious violations of this Code.

(2) Examples may include:

a) deliberate and substantial betrayal of Brotherly Trust;

b) knowingly placing another Brother in serious danger;

c) systematic exploitation of fellow Brothers;

d) serious abuse of confidential information;

e) repeated grave violations demonstrating an unwillingness to respect the Brotherhood.

(3) The Council may recommend suspension or termination of membership.

(4) No Brother shall be declared guilty solely on the basis of rumor or unsupported accusation.

§ 49. Aggravating Circumstances

(1) The following circumstances may increase the seriousness of an offence:

a) deliberate intent;

b) repeated misconduct;

c) abuse of a position of trust;

d) exploitation of a vulnerable Brother;

e) refusal to acknowledge responsibility;

f) causing significant harm.

(2) The Council shall consider the totality of the circumstances.

§ 50. Mitigating Circumstances

(1) The following circumstances may reduce the seriousness of an offence:

a) genuine misunderstanding;

b) absence of deliberate intent;

c) voluntary admission of responsibility;

d) immediate efforts to repair the damage;

e) extraordinary personal circumstances;

f) a previously demonstrated record of reliability.

(2) No mitigating circumstance shall automatically eliminate responsibility.

§ 51. Attempted Violations

(1) An attempted violation may be treated as an offence even where the intended result was not achieved.

(2) The Council shall distinguish between preparation, attempt, and completed misconduct.

(3) The severity of the disciplinary measure shall reflect the actual circumstances.

TITLE VIII — PENALTIES AND DISCIPLINARY PROCEEDINGS

§ 52. General Principles of Punishment

(1) Disciplinary measures shall be fair, proportionate, and consistent.

(2) The purpose of punishment shall be correction, restoration of trust, and protection of the Brotherhood.

(3) Punishment shall never involve humiliation, intimidation, physical harm, or dangerous activities.

(4) No Brother shall be punished twice for the same offence without a separate and justified reason.

§ 53. Available Disciplinary Measures

The Council may impose the following measures:

a) verbal warning;

b) formal written warning;

c) apology to the affected Brother;

d) restitution of damaged or lost property;

e) temporary suspension of privileges;

f) probationary membership;

g) temporary suspension of membership;

h) termination of membership.

§ 54. Verbal Warning

(1) A verbal warning may be issued for a minor offence.

(2) The warning shall identify the conduct requiring correction.

(3) A verbal warning shall not automatically constitute a formal disciplinary record.

§ 55. Formal Written Warning

(1) A written warning may be issued where misconduct is repeated or sufficiently serious.

(2) The warning shall specify the relevant provision of this Code.

(3) The Brother shall be informed of the conduct expected from him in the future.

§ 56. Mandatory Apology

(1) Where a violation has caused personal harm or damaged trust, the Council may require an apology.

(2) The apology shall acknowledge the conduct and its consequences.

(3) An apology shall not require the affected Brother to forgive the offender.

§ 57. Restitution

(1) A Brother responsible for damage or loss may be required to provide reasonable restitution.

(2) Restitution may include:

a) returning borrowed property;

b) repairing damaged property;

c) replacing lost items;

d) repaying agreed financial obligations.

(3) Restitution shall not exceed the reasonable extent of the damage.

§ 58. Probationary Membership

(1) Probationary membership may be imposed following a serious violation.

(2) During probation, the Brother shall remain a recognized member but may be subject to additional review.

(3) The Council shall establish a reasonable probationary period.

(4) Successful completion shall restore the Brother's ordinary membership status.

§ 59. Temporary Suspension

(1) Temporary suspension may be imposed where continued membership privileges present a genuine risk to the Brotherhood.

(2) The suspension shall be limited to a defined period.

(3) The suspended Brother shall retain the right to receive notice of relevant proceedings.

(4) Suspension shall not prevent the Brother from seeking reconsideration.

§ 60. Termination of Membership

(1) Membership may be terminated only for grave or repeated serious violations.

(2) Termination shall require a formal hearing.

(3) The affected Brother shall receive notice of the allegations and an opportunity to respond.

(4) The Council shall issue a written decision explaining its reasoning.

(5) Termination shall be considered a measure of last resort.

TITLE IX — APPEALS, PARDONS AND RESTORATION OF MEMBERSHIP

§ 61. Right of Appeal

(1) Every Brother subject to a disciplinary decision shall possess the right to appeal.

(2) An appeal may challenge:

a) the factual findings of the Council;

b) the interpretation of the relevant provision;

c) the proportionality of the disciplinary measure;

d) the fairness of the proceedings.

(3) No Brother shall be denied the right to appeal merely because the original decision was unanimous.

§ 62. Submission of an Appeal

(1) An appeal shall be submitted within fourteen days of the original decision.

(2) The appeal shall identify the decision being challenged.

(3) The appealing Brother should provide reasons supporting reconsideration.

(4) The Council may accept a late appeal where reasonable circumstances justify the delay.

§ 63. Review of the Original Decision

(1) An appeal shall be reviewed by Council members who were not directly involved in the original dispute, where reasonably possible.

(2) The reviewing members shall examine the evidence and arguments presented.

(3) The original decision may be:

a) upheld;

b) modified;

c) overturned;

d) returned for a new hearing.

§ 64. Finality of Decisions

(1) A decision reached following the appeal procedure shall ordinarily be final.

(2) Reopening a concluded matter shall require significant new evidence or proof of serious procedural unfairness.

(3) The Council shall not reopen a case merely because a Brother dislikes its outcome.

§ 65. The Right to Pardon

(1) A Brother who has committed a violation may request a pardon.

(2) A pardon may be granted where:

a) responsibility has been acknowledged;

b) reasonable efforts have been made to repair the damage;

c) the affected Brother has been given an opportunity to express his position;

d) the Council considers restoration appropriate.

(3) A pardon shall not be automatic.

§ 66. Forgiveness and Legal Pardon

(1) Forgiveness shall remain a personal decision of the affected Brother.

(2) The Council may restore membership privileges without requiring personal forgiveness.

(3) No Brother shall be forced to forgive another Brother.

(4) A pardon shall not erase the historical fact that a violation occurred.

§ 67. Restoration of Membership

(1) A suspended or former Brother may request restoration of membership.

(2) Restoration shall depend upon:

a) demonstrated improvement in conduct;

b) fulfillment of outstanding obligations;

c) respect for the rights of other Brothers;

d) the absence of ongoing misconduct.

(3) The Council may impose reasonable conditions upon restoration.

§ 68. Rehabilitation

(1) A Brother who has completed his disciplinary obligations shall be entitled to fair consideration without indefinite punishment.

(2) Past misconduct shall not be used as an automatic justification for permanent exclusion.

(3) The Brotherhood shall recognize genuine improvement.

TITLE X — CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS AND LEGISLATIVE AUTHORITY

§ 69. Supremacy of the Constitution

(1) This Book of Laws shall constitute the principal legal framework of the Brotherhood.

(2) All subordinate rules, traditions, and Council decisions shall be consistent with its provisions.

(3) No informal tradition shall override an express constitutional protection.

§ 70. Authority to Propose Amendments

(1) Any recognized Brother may propose an amendment to this Code.

(2) A proposal shall identify:

a) the provision to be amended;

b) the proposed replacement or additional wording;

c) the reason for the amendment.

(3) The Council shall review all properly submitted proposals.

§ 71. Legislative Review

(1) Proposed amendments shall be discussed before voting.

(2) Every Brother affected by a proposed amendment shall have a reasonable opportunity to express his opinion.

(3) Amendments shall not be adopted secretly.

§ 72. Adoption of Amendments

(1) Ordinary amendments shall require approval by a two-thirds majority of eligible voting members.

(2) Amendments affecting fundamental rights shall require stronger approval as determined by the Council.

(3) No amendment shall retroactively criminalize conduct that was not prohibited when committed.

§ 73. Publication of Amendments

(1) Every adopted amendment shall be entered into the official edition of the Code.

(2) The amended provision shall identify its revision date.

(3) Previous versions shall be preserved for historical reference.

§ 74. Emergency Amendments

(1) Temporary amendments may be adopted where an extraordinary circumstance threatens the stability of the Brotherhood.

(2) Such amendments shall require a two-thirds majority.

(3) Emergency amendments shall expire after thirty days unless formally approved through the ordinary legislative procedure.

(4) No emergency amendment may abolish the fundamental right to a fair hearing.

§ 75. Interpretation of Ambiguous Provisions

(1) Where a provision is unclear, the Council shall interpret it according to the purpose and principles of this Code.

(2) Interpretation shall favor fairness over technical exploitation.

(3) No Brother shall be punished solely on the basis of an interpretation that could not reasonably have been anticipated.

TITLE XI — SPECIAL BROTHERHOOD INSTITUTIONS

§ 76. The Office of the Wingman

(1) The Office of the Wingman is hereby recognized as an honorary institution of the Brotherhood.

(2) Its purpose shall be to provide encouragement, support, and honest assistance to Brothers navigating social situations.

(3) A Wingman shall:

a) respect the wishes of the Brother receiving assistance;

b) provide honest advice;

c) avoid deliberate embarrassment;

d) respect the autonomy of all persons involved.

§ 77. The Wingman Oath

(1) A Brother accepting the role of Wingman shall act with loyalty and good judgment.

(2) The Wingman shall not invent false claims about another person.

(3) The Wingman shall not pressure anyone into romantic or social situations.

(4) The Wingman shall recognize that the objective is mutual respect, not guaranteed success.

§ 78. The Office of the Designated Driver

(1) The Office of the Designated Driver shall be recognized as a safety institution.

(2) Its purpose shall be to ensure that Brothers can travel safely.

(3) The designated driver shall not operate a vehicle after consuming alcohol or any substance that impairs driving.

(4) Every Brother shall respect the driver's responsibility.

(5) No Brother shall pressure another to drive when that person is tired, unwell, or otherwise unable to drive safely.

§ 79. The Keeper of Secrets

(1) The Keeper of Secrets shall be an honorary position assigned to a Brother recognized for discretion and reliability.

(2) The Keeper shall respect confidential information entrusted to him.

(3) Confidentiality shall not require concealing serious wrongdoing or an immediate threat to someone's safety.

(4) The Keeper shall not disclose private information for entertainment, gossip, or personal advantage.

§ 80. The Archivist of the Brotherhood

(1) The Archivist shall maintain records of significant Brotherhood events.

(2) The Archivist may preserve:

a) important photographs;

b) memorable achievements;

c) official decisions;

d) historical traditions;

e) notable Brotherhood gatherings.

(3) Private information shall not be archived or distributed without appropriate permission.

§ 81. The Treasurer

(1) The Brotherhood may appoint a Treasurer to oversee shared funds.

(2) The Treasurer shall maintain accurate financial records.

(3) Shared funds shall only be used for purposes approved by the contributing Brothers.

(4) The Treasurer shall provide a transparent account of expenditures upon reasonable request.

(5) No Brother shall use shared funds for personal benefit without authorization.

TITLE XII — SACRED TRADITIONS AND CEREMONIAL LAW

§ 82. Recognition of Brotherhood Traditions

(1) Traditions shall serve to strengthen friendship, unity, and shared identity.

(2) Traditions may be established through repeated voluntary participation.

(3) No tradition shall be considered binding merely because it is old.

§ 83. The Annual Brotherhood Gathering

(1) The Brotherhood may establish an annual gathering known as the Brothers' Assembly.

(2) The Assembly shall serve as an opportunity to:

a) celebrate shared achievements;

b) welcome new Brothers;

c) settle outstanding matters;

d) remember significant events;

e) renew the commitment to the Code.

§ 84. The Brotherhood Toast

(1) A ceremonial toast may be performed at official gatherings.

(2) Participation shall be voluntary.

(3) The toast may be made with any suitable beverage, including a non-alcoholic one.

(4) No Brother shall be required to consume alcohol to demonstrate loyalty.

§ 85. The Rule of the Last Slice

(1) Where a shared food item remains and ownership is unclear, the Last Slice Rule shall apply.

(2) A Brother wishing to claim the final portion shall first make his intention known.

(3) Where multiple Brothers claim the final portion, the matter shall be resolved through mutual agreement or a fair method of selection.

(4) Secretly taking the final portion after another Brother has expressly reserved it shall constitute a minor offence.

§ 86. The Sacred Couch Principle

(1) During Brotherhood gatherings, reasonable consideration shall be given to seating arrangements.

(2) A Brother who has temporarily left his seat shall not automatically lose his place.

(3) A seat may be considered abandoned where the Brother has clearly indicated that he has finished using it.

(4) The Sacred Couch Principle shall not override accessibility needs or reasonable comfort.

§ 87. The Rule of the Group Photograph

(1) Every Brother may decline to appear in a photograph.

(2) No photograph shall be published or distributed in a manner that violates another person's reasonable privacy expectations.

(3) The Brotherhood shall preserve memorable moments without disregarding individual consent.

§ 88. The Final Word

(1) The traditions of the Brotherhood shall never become more important than the Brothers themselves.

(2) Loyalty shall not require blind obedience.

(3) Brotherhood shall be measured by conduct, not by titles, ceremonies, or declarations.

TITLE XIII — DIGITAL CONDUCT AND TECHNOLOGICAL RESPONSIBILITIES

§ 89. General Principles of Digital Brotherhood

(1) The principles of loyalty, honesty, and respect shall apply equally in digital communication.

(2) A Brother shall not use technology to undermine the trust of another Brother.

(3) Digital misconduct shall be subject to the same general standards of responsibility as conduct in person.

§ 90. Protection of Private Messages

(1) Private messages exchanged between Brothers shall be treated as confidential.

(2) No Brother shall publish, forward, or distribute private conversations without reasonable permission.

(3) Exceptions may apply where disclosure is necessary to address serious misconduct or protect someone's safety.

§ 91. The Screenshot Principle

(1) A screenshot of a private conversation shall not automatically become public property.

(2) A Brother shall not use screenshots to embarrass, manipulate, or threaten another Brother.

(3) The selective removal of context for the purpose of misleading others shall constitute a breach of trust.

§ 92. Group Chat Membership

(1) Official Brotherhood group chats shall be maintained for communication and coordination.

(2) No Brother shall be removed from a group chat merely to humiliate him.

(3) Temporary removal may be justified where necessary to resolve serious disruption.

(4) A Brother who leaves a group chat voluntarily shall not automatically forfeit his membership.

§ 93. The Right to Be Left on Read

(1) Every Brother shall retain the right to respond to messages at a reasonable time of his choosing.

(2) Failure to respond immediately shall not constitute betrayal.

(3) However, messages concerning urgent arrangements or genuine emergencies should receive appropriate attention.

§ 94. Digital Identity and Accounts

(1) No Brother shall access another Brother's personal account without permission.

(2) Passwords, authentication codes, and private credentials shall not be requested as proof of loyalty.

(3) Impersonation of another Brother without consent shall constitute a violation of this Code.

§ 95. Online Reputation

(1) No Brother shall knowingly spread false information about another Brother online.

(2) Public criticism shall remain proportionate and truthful.

(3) Disagreements should, where reasonably possible, be resolved privately before being turned into public entertainment.

§ 96. Gaming Conduct

(1) Brotherhood gaming sessions shall be governed by fair play and mutual respect.

(2) A Brother shall not deliberately sabotage another Brother's performance for personal amusement.

(3) Friendly competition shall be encouraged.

(4) Losing a match shall not justify insults, threats, or destruction of shared property.

§ 97. The Controller Protection Act

(1) No Brother shall intentionally damage another Brother's controller, keyboard, headset, or other gaming equipment.

(2) Borrowed equipment shall be treated with reasonable care.

(3) Rage shall not constitute a valid legal defense.

§ 98. Digital Memories

(1) Photographs, recordings, and other digital memories of Brotherhood events may be preserved with appropriate permission.

(2) A Brother shall not deliberately alter such material to create a false impression of another Brother's conduct.

(3) Embarrassing material shall not be published merely because it is considered humorous by the person publishing it.

TITLE XIV — SOCIAL GATHERINGS, PUBLIC CONDUCT AND REPUTATION

§ 99. General Standard of Public Conduct

(1) Every Brother shall conduct himself with reasonable respect in public.

(2) No Brother shall deliberately create unnecessary embarrassment for another Brother.

(3) A Brother shall not be expected to participate in conduct that violates his personal boundaries.

§ 100. The Rule of the Entrance

(1) A Brother arriving at a gathering shall make a reasonable effort to acknowledge the Brothers already present.

(2) The remaining Brothers should make reasonable efforts to welcome him.

(3) No Brother shall be deliberately ignored as a means of humiliation.

§ 101. The Rule of Group Invitations

(1) Invitations to Brotherhood events shall be communicated clearly.

(2) No Brother shall deliberately mislead another Brother about the time, location, or nature of an event.

(3) An invitation shall not create an obligation to attend.

§ 102. The Rule of the Missing Brother

(1) Where a Brother unexpectedly disappears from a gathering, reasonable efforts shall be made to establish his safety.

(2) Such efforts shall be proportionate to the circumstances.

(3) No Brother shall be abandoned in an unfamiliar or unsafe location merely for the sake of convenience.

§ 103. Public Disagreements

(1) Brothers shall avoid unnecessary public arguments.

(2) A Brother may disagree openly without being considered disloyal.

(3) No Brother shall be required to support an obviously false statement merely to preserve appearances.

§ 104. Protection of Public Reputation

(1) No Brother shall knowingly damage another Brother's reputation through fabricated allegations.

(2) Honest criticism shall remain permissible.

(3) Deliberate humiliation shall constitute misconduct where it causes significant and unjustified harm.

§ 105. The Rule of the Awkward Encounter

(1) Where a Brother encounters an uncomfortable social situation, another Brother may offer reasonable assistance.

(2) Such assistance may include changing the subject, initiating a conversation, or providing a respectful opportunity to leave.

(3) Assistance shall not involve deception that places another person at risk.

§ 106. The Rule of the Shared Journey

(1) Brothers travelling together shall communicate significant changes to their plans.

(2) No Brother shall knowingly leave another Brother without a reasonable way to return home.

(3) Travel arrangements shall take into account safety, accessibility, and individual circumstances.

§ 107. The Rule of Public Loyalty

(1) A Brother shall not be required to defend misconduct merely because the person responsible is his friend.

(2) Loyalty shall include the willingness to correct a Brother privately and honestly.

(3) Public support shall never require abandoning personal integrity.

TITLE XV — TRAVEL, ADVENTURES AND BROTHERHOOD EXPEDITIONS

§ 108. Establishment of Brotherhood Expeditions

(1) A Brotherhood Expedition shall mean a voluntary journey, trip, excursion, or organized adventure undertaken by two or more Brothers.

(2) Every Expedition shall have a reasonable purpose, whether recreational, educational, cultural, or spontaneous.

(3) No Expedition shall require a Brother to undertake unreasonable risks.

§ 109. The Expedition Organizer

(1) An Expedition may have an appointed Organizer.

(2) The Organizer shall be responsible for communicating:

a) the destination;

b) the expected schedule;

c) the approximate costs;

d) relevant transportation arrangements;

e) necessary safety information.

(3) The Organizer shall not possess authority to force participation.

§ 110. Financial Transparency During Travel

(1) Shared travel expenses shall be communicated before they are incurred whenever reasonably possible.

(2) No Brother shall knowingly impose undisclosed expenses upon another Brother.

(3) Shared costs shall be divided according to prior agreement or a fair arrangement.

§ 111. The Rule of the Lost Brother

(1) Where a Brother becomes separated from the group, reasonable efforts shall be made to re-establish contact.

(2) The group shall agree upon a meeting point whenever separation is reasonably foreseeable.

(3) A Brother shall not be mocked for becoming lost where he has made a genuine effort to follow the agreed arrangements.

§ 112. Accommodation and Personal Space

(1) Shared accommodation shall respect the reasonable privacy of every Brother.

(2) No Brother shall be forced to share a room or sleeping arrangement against his wishes.

(3) Personal belongings shall not be used without permission.

(4) Disagreements concerning accommodation shall be resolved before they become unnecessary disputes.

§ 113. The Rule of the Spontaneous Adventure

(1) Spontaneous plans shall be permitted and encouraged.

(2) Spontaneity shall not excuse reckless disregard for safety, money, or prior commitments.

(3) Every Brother shall retain the right to decline an adventure without being accused of betraying the Brotherhood.

§ 114. The Rule of the Return Journey

(1) Before the conclusion of an Expedition, reasonable return arrangements shall be established.

(2) A Brother who requires assistance returning home shall not be deliberately ignored.

(3) The Organizer shall communicate any significant changes to the return schedule.

§ 115. Preservation of Expedition Memories

(1) Significant Brotherhood Expeditions may be recorded in the official historical archive.

(2) The archive may include photographs, dates, locations, and notable events.

(3) No Brother shall be required to participate in photographs or recordings.

TITLE XVI — DISPUTES, RIVALRIES AND THE ART OF RECONCILIATION

§ 116. Recognition of Disputes

(1) Disagreements between Brothers shall be recognized as a normal part of Brotherhood.

(2) The existence of a disagreement shall not automatically constitute a violation of this Code.

(3) Honest disagreement shall be distinguished from deliberate hostility.

§ 117. The Right to Be Heard

(1) Every Brother involved in a dispute shall have an opportunity to explain his position.

(2) No Brother shall be declared responsible solely because he is less persuasive or less outspoken.

(3) The parties shall be encouraged to communicate directly where appropriate.

§ 118. The First Attempt at Reconciliation

(1) Before requesting formal intervention by the Council, Brothers should attempt reasonable reconciliation.

(2) Such an attempt may include:

a) a direct conversation;

b) clarification of misunderstandings;

c) acknowledgment of mistakes;

d) a mutually agreed compromise.

(3) This requirement shall not apply where direct contact would be unsafe or inappropriate.

§ 119. The Neutral Mediator

(1) The parties may agree to appoint a neutral Brother as mediator.

(2) The mediator shall not impose a decision.

(3) The mediator shall assist the parties in identifying the disagreement and considering possible solutions.

(4) The mediator shall remain impartial.

§ 120. Friendly Rivalries

(1) Friendly rivalries shall be permitted as a form of entertainment and mutual motivation.

(2) Such rivalries may concern:

a) gaming;

b) sports;

c) academic achievements;

d) creative projects;

e) harmless competitions.

(3) Rivalries shall not justify harassment, humiliation, or deliberate sabotage.

§ 121. The Rule of the Lost Bet

(1) A Brother who voluntarily accepts a reasonable bet shall respect the agreed outcome.

(2) The terms of the bet shall be established before the result is known.

(3) No bet shall require illegal, dangerous, humiliating, or otherwise harmful conduct.

(4) A bet shall not become enforceable merely because it was announced loudly.

§ 122. The Rule of the Apology

(1) A Brother who recognizes that he has caused unjustified harm should offer a sincere apology.

(2) An apology shall not be treated as an admission to allegations that are demonstrably false.

(3) The receiving Brother shall be free to decide whether to accept the apology.

§ 123. Restoration of Trust

(1) Trust shall be restored through consistent conduct rather than promises alone.

(2) A Brother seeking reconciliation shall demonstrate reliability over time.

(3) The affected Brother shall not be required to restore trust immediately.

§ 124. The Final Principle of Reconciliation

(1) The purpose of resolving disputes shall be to preserve dignity and restore mutual respect wherever possible.

(2) Winning an argument shall not automatically constitute winning the dispute.

(3) The Brotherhood shall value lasting friendship above temporary victory.

TITLE XVII — ROMANTIC RELATIONSHIPS AND THE RIGHTS OF THIRD PARTIES

§ 125. General Principles of Romantic Conduct

(1) Every person shall retain full autonomy over his or her romantic decisions.

(2) No Brother shall claim ownership over another person's affection, attention, or relationship choices.

(3) Loyalty between Brothers shall be maintained without disregarding the dignity and independence of third parties.

§ 126. The Former Partner Principle

(1) A Brother shall exercise particular consideration before pursuing a romantic relationship with the former partner of another Brother.

(2) Such conduct shall require honest communication with the affected Brother.

(3) No Brother shall deliberately conceal such intentions for the purpose of avoiding an uncomfortable conversation.

(4) The former partner shall retain the right to make an independent decision.

§ 127. The Right to Move On

(1) A Brother shall not be required to remain emotionally attached to a former relationship.

(2) No Brother shall be mocked for expressing sadness, disappointment, or uncertainty following a breakup.

(3) The Brotherhood shall provide reasonable support without imposing a particular recovery schedule.

§ 128. The No-Sabotage Principle

(1) No Brother shall deliberately sabotage another Brother's genuine romantic opportunity.

(2) Prohibited conduct may include:

a) knowingly spreading false information;

b) intentionally creating misunderstandings;

c) pretending to represent another Brother without permission;

d) deliberately interfering with agreed plans.

(3) Honest advice and respectful disagreement shall not constitute sabotage.

§ 129. The Wingman Limitation

(1) A Wingman shall assist only within the boundaries established by the people involved.

(2) No Wingman shall fabricate personal information or misrepresent another person's intentions.

(3) The Wingman shall respect rejection immediately.

(4) The success of a Wingman shall be measured by his conduct, not by the outcome of a romantic interaction.

§ 130. The Rule of the Double Date

(1) A Brother invited to participate in a double date shall respect the arrangements and boundaries of all participants.

(2) No Brother shall deliberately interfere with another person's conversation or relationship.

(3) A double date shall not create an obligation for anyone to pursue a relationship.

§ 131. The Lemon Law

(1) Where a Brother has participated in a romantic introduction arranged by another Brother, the introducing Brother shall make reasonable efforts to ensure that the introduction was represented honestly.

(2) No Brother shall be blamed for another person's independent choices.

(3) The Council may recognize the Lemon Law as an honorary principle of romantic matchmaking.

§ 132. The Right to Refuse

(1) Every person shall possess an unconditional right to refuse romantic attention.

(2) No Brother shall pressure, threaten, manipulate, or repeatedly pursue a person who has clearly declined.

(3) Respecting rejection shall constitute an essential obligation of Brotherhood.

§ 133. Romantic Confidentiality

(1) Private romantic conversations entrusted to a Brother shall be treated with discretion.

(2) No Brother shall disclose intimate personal information for entertainment or social advantage.

(3) Confidentiality shall not require concealing serious threats, coercion, or abuse.

TITLE XVIII — TIME, COMMITMENTS AND BROTHERHOOD RESPONSIBILITIES

§ 134. The Principle of Punctuality

(1) A Brother shall make a reasonable effort to arrive at agreed times.

(2) Where delay becomes unavoidable, the affected Brothers shall be informed.

(3) Repeated unexplained lateness may constitute a moderate offence.

§ 135. The Rule of the Five-Minute Warning

(1) A Brother who becomes aware that he will be late should provide notice as soon as reasonably possible.

(2) The expression "I'm almost there" shall not be used where the Brother has not yet begun travelling.

(3) The Council recognizes that traffic, public transportation, and other unforeseen circumstances may justify delays.

§ 136. The Protection of Existing Plans

(1) An accepted commitment shall not be cancelled without reasonable justification.

(2) A more entertaining alternative shall not automatically constitute sufficient justification.

(3) Repeatedly abandoning plans at the last moment may constitute a breach of reliability.

§ 137. The Rule of the Calendar

(1) Important Brotherhood events should be communicated in advance.

(2) A Brother shall make reasonable efforts to remember significant commitments.

(3) Forgetting a date shall not automatically constitute betrayal, provided the Brother acts in good faith.

§ 138. The Right to Personal Time

(1) Every Brother shall retain the right to spend time alone.

(2) Refusal of an invitation shall not automatically be interpreted as rejection of the Brotherhood.

(3) No Brother shall be required to justify every personal decision.

§ 139. The Rule of the Emergency Call

(1) A Brother receiving a genuine emergency request shall make reasonable efforts to assist.

(2) The nature of the assistance shall depend upon the circumstances and the Brother's actual ability to help.

(3) No Brother shall be expected to perform an impossible or unsafe act.

§ 140. The Rule of the Important Day

(1) Brothers should make reasonable efforts to acknowledge important personal milestones.

(2) Such milestones may include:

a) birthdays;

b) examinations;

c) graduations;

d) major achievements;

e) significant personal events.

(3) The value of recognition shall not be measured solely by the financial value of a gift.

§ 141. The Rule of the Unfinished Plan

(1) Where a Brotherhood plan remains incomplete, the Brothers involved should establish whether it remains active.

(2) No Brother shall indefinitely rely upon an arrangement that has never been confirmed.

(3) A plan shall not become official merely because someone mentioned it at 2 a.m.

TITLE XIX — EDUCATION, CAREERS AND PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT

§ 142. The Duty of Mutual Encouragement

(1) Brothers shall encourage one another to pursue meaningful personal goals.

(2) No Brother shall deliberately discourage another Brother merely because of jealousy or insecurity.

(3) Honest criticism shall remain permissible where intended to provide constructive assistance.

§ 143. Academic Assistance

(1) Brothers may provide reasonable assistance with educational responsibilities.

(2) Such assistance may include:

a) explaining difficult concepts;

b) sharing permitted study materials;

c) preparing for examinations;

d) reviewing assignments;

e) offering organizational advice.

(3) Assistance shall not require participation in academic dishonesty.

§ 144. The Examination Protection Principle

(1) A Brother preparing for an important examination shall receive reasonable consideration.

(2) Other Brothers should avoid unnecessary distractions where they are aware of the examination schedule.

(3) No Brother shall be mocked for prioritizing education over entertainment.

§ 145. Career Opportunities

(1) Brothers may share relevant employment, internship, and professional opportunities.

(2) A Brother shall not knowingly conceal a valuable opportunity solely to prevent another Brother from succeeding.

(3) Recommendations shall be honest and based on genuine knowledge.

§ 146. The Rule of Constructive Competition

(1) Competition between Brothers shall be permitted and encouraged where it promotes improvement.

(2) A Brother's success shall not automatically be interpreted as another Brother's failure.

(3) Jealousy shall not justify deliberate obstruction.

§ 147. Personal Development

(1) Every Brother shall retain the freedom to develop different interests, abilities, and ambitions.

(2) The Brotherhood shall respect changes in personal priorities.

(3) No Brother shall be required to remain the same person merely to preserve an old group dynamic.

§ 148. The Rule of the First Attempt

(1) A Brother attempting something new shall be entitled to reasonable encouragement.

(2) Failure shall not constitute grounds for humiliation.

(3) Genuine effort shall be recognized independently of the final result.

§ 149. Protection Against Unfair Comparison

(1) No Brother shall deliberately diminish another Brother's achievements through constant comparison.

(2) Different circumstances and starting points shall be acknowledged.

(3) The Brotherhood shall recognize individual progress.

TITLE XX — THE CODE OF HONOR AND EXCEPTIONAL CIRCUMSTANCES

§ 150. The Fundamental Code of Honor

(1) Every Brother shall act with integrity, even when no other Brother is present.

(2) The absence of witnesses shall not eliminate responsibility.

(3) Honor shall be demonstrated through consistent conduct rather than declarations.

§ 151. The Rule of Truth

(1) A Brother shall not knowingly deceive another Brother in a matter of significant importance.

(2) Minor social exaggerations shall be distinguished from deliberate deception causing meaningful harm.

(3) The truth shall not be used as an excuse for unnecessary cruelty.

§ 152. The Rule of Keeping One's Word

(1) A Brother shall make reasonable efforts to honor promises voluntarily made.

(2) Where circumstances prevent fulfillment, the affected Brother shall be informed.

(3) A promise shall not be considered binding where it was obtained through coercion or deception.

§ 153. The Protection of Vulnerable Brothers

(1) No Brother shall exploit another Brother's difficult personal circumstances.

(2) Particular consideration shall be given where a Brother is experiencing significant stress, grief, uncertainty, or hardship.

(3) Requests for assistance shall be treated with dignity.

§ 154. Exceptional Circumstances

(1) The Council may recognize exceptional circumstances where strict application of an ordinary provision would produce an unreasonable result.

(2) Such circumstances may include:

a) emergencies;

b) serious illness;

c) unexpected family responsibilities;

d) unavoidable financial hardship;

e) extraordinary external events.

(3) Recognition of exceptional circumstances shall not automatically eliminate all responsibility.

§ 155. The Principle of Common Sense

(1) No provision of this Code shall be interpreted in a manner that produces an obviously absurd result.

(2) Common sense shall be considered alongside the written text.

(3) The Council shall prefer reasonable interpretation over unnecessary technicality.

§ 156. The Rule Against Blind Loyalty

(1) Loyalty shall not require a Brother to defend conduct that is clearly harmful or dishonest.

(2) A Brother shall be permitted to disagree with another Brother without forfeiting his standing.

(3) True loyalty shall include honest correction.

§ 157. The Highest Duty

(1) The highest duty of every Brother shall be to protect the dignity, safety, and trust of the Brotherhood.

(2) No tradition, ceremony, competition, or informal rule shall override this duty.

(3) Every Brother shall remember that the Code exists to serve the Brotherhood, and not the other way around.

TITLE XXI — BROTHERHOOD HIERARCHY, SENIORITY AND LEADERSHIP

§ 158. Equality of Brothers

(1) Every recognized Brother shall possess equal fundamental status within the Brotherhood.

(2) No Brother shall claim superiority solely on the basis of wealth, popularity, appearance, academic achievement, or social influence.

(3) Differences in experience may justify additional responsibilities but shall not create absolute authority.

§ 159. Seniority

(1) Seniority may be recognized according to the length of a Brother's membership.

(2) Seniority shall entitle a Brother to respect for his experience.

(3) Seniority shall not entitle a Brother to immunity from this Code.

(4) An older Brother shall not automatically be considered wiser.

§ 160. The Founding Brothers

(1) Brothers responsible for establishing the Brotherhood may be recognized as Founding Brothers.

(2) Founding Brothers may preserve the original traditions and historical records.

(3) Founding status shall not grant unlimited authority over other members.

(4) The Founding Brothers shall remain subject to the same fundamental obligations as every other Brother.

§ 161. Leadership Responsibilities

(1) A Brother entrusted with leadership shall act in the interests of the Brotherhood.

(2) Leadership shall be based upon responsibility rather than privilege.

(3) A leader shall:

a) listen to the Brothers;

b) communicate decisions clearly;

c) avoid favoritism;

d) accept reasonable criticism;

e) acknowledge mistakes.

§ 162. Abuse of Authority

(1) No Brother shall use a leadership position to intimidate, manipulate, or exploit another Brother.

(2) Decisions made for personal benefit shall be subject to review.

(3) Abuse of authority may constitute a serious offence.

§ 163. The Rule of the First Among Equals

(1) Where a gathering requires a temporary leader, the Brothers may appoint one Brother as First Among Equals.

(2) Such appointment shall be limited to the specific purpose for which it was made.

(3) The appointed Brother shall not possess authority over unrelated personal matters.

§ 164. Succession of Leadership

(1) Where a leader resigns, becomes unavailable, or loses the confidence of the Brotherhood, a successor may be appointed.

(2) The successor shall be selected through a fair process.

(3) No Brother shall automatically inherit leadership merely because he is the loudest person in the room.

TITLE XXII — PROPERTY, SHARED RESOURCES AND BORROWING

§ 165. The Principle of Personal Ownership

(1) Every Brother shall retain exclusive control over his personal property.

(2) Friendship shall not create automatic permission to use another Brother's belongings.

(3) Borrowing shall require consent.

§ 166. The Rule of Returning Borrowed Property

(1) Borrowed property shall be returned within the agreed period.

(2) Where no period has been agreed, the property shall be returned within a reasonable time.

(3) A Brother shall not require repeated reminders to return something that does not belong to him.

§ 167. The Rule of Borrowed Clothing

(1) Clothing borrowed from another Brother shall be returned in reasonable condition.

(2) The borrowing Brother shall not permanently adopt the item merely because it looks better on him.

(3) The phrase "I'll give it back next time" shall not constitute an indefinite extension.

§ 168. Shared Food and Beverages

(1) Food or beverages explicitly offered for sharing may be consumed by participating Brothers.

(2) Personal food shall not automatically become communal property.

(3) Where ownership is unclear, permission shall be requested.

(4) The last portion shall remain subject to § 85 of this Code.

§ 169. The Rule of the Phone Charger

(1) A Brother lending a phone charger shall be entitled to its return.

(2) The borrower shall not leave the charger in an unknown location.

(3) A charger shall not be considered a permanent donation merely because it was borrowed during an emergency.

§ 170. The Rule of the Open Tab

(1) A Brother who agrees to cover a shared expense shall communicate the amount and expectations clearly.

(2) No Brother shall exploit informal financial arrangements.

(3) Outstanding debts shall be settled within a reasonable period.

§ 171. Shared Equipment

(1) Equipment purchased jointly shall be treated as jointly owned.

(2) Decisions concerning its use, maintenance, and disposal shall be made by agreement.

(3) No Brother shall sell or permanently remove jointly owned property without the consent of the other owners.

§ 172. Digital Purchases and Shared Subscriptions

(1) Shared digital purchases shall be governed by the original agreement between the participating Brothers.

(2) No Brother shall change shared account credentials without reasonable notice.

(3) Subscription costs shall be communicated transparently.

(4) No Brother shall claim ownership over a digital purchase merely because he remembers the password.

TITLE XXIII — CELEBRATIONS, PARTIES AND SPECIAL OCCASIONS

§ 173. Recognition of Special Occasions

(1) The Brotherhood may recognize birthdays, graduations, achievements, holidays, and other significant events.

(2) Participation in celebrations shall remain voluntary.

(3) The importance of an occasion shall be determined by its personal significance rather than its financial cost.

§ 174. The Birthday Rule

(1) A Brother's birthday shall be considered a recognized Brotherhood occasion.

(2) Other Brothers should make a reasonable effort to acknowledge the occasion.

(3) Forgetting a birthday shall not automatically constitute betrayal.

(4) Repeatedly forgetting the same Brother's birthday while remembering everyone else's may be considered suspicious.

§ 175. The Birthday Celebration Authority

(1) The birthday Brother shall ordinarily have reasonable influence over the celebration's general arrangements.

(2) Other Brothers may suggest alternatives.

(3) No celebration shall require spending beyond a Brother's reasonable means.

§ 176. The Rule of the Group Gift

(1) Group gifts shall be voluntary.

(2) Contributions shall be agreed upon in advance where possible.

(3) No Brother shall be publicly shamed for declining or being unable to contribute.

(4) The gift shall reflect consideration rather than financial competition.

§ 177. The Rule of the Unexpected Guest

(1) A Brother organizing a private gathering shall communicate relevant guest arrangements.

(2) No Brother shall invite additional guests into another person's home without permission.

(3) The host shall retain the right to establish reasonable conditions for attendance.

§ 178. The Rule of the Host

(1) A host shall make reasonable efforts to provide a welcoming environment.

(2) Guests shall respect the host's property and household rules.

(3) A Brother shall not treat another Brother's home as a public venue.

§ 179. The Rule of the Last One Leaving

(1) Brothers remaining at a gathering shall respect the host's reasonable wishes concerning its conclusion.

(2) No Brother shall be expected to remain indefinitely.

(3) Where assistance with cleaning or organizing has been agreed upon, participating Brothers shall make reasonable efforts to fulfill that agreement.

§ 180. The Rule of the Morning After

(1) Following a major Brotherhood event, reasonable consideration shall be given to Brothers who require rest or assistance.

(2) No Brother shall be pressured to participate in further activities.

(3) Safety and personal well-being shall take priority over continuing the celebration.

§ 181. The Rule of the Legendary Story

(1) A memorable Brotherhood event may be recorded as a Legendary Story.

(2) The story shall preserve the essential facts while allowing harmless humor.

(3) No story shall be used to expose private information or deliberately humiliate a Brother.

TITLE XXIV — THE PLAYBOOK, LEGENDARY RULES AND HISTORICAL PRECEDENTS

§ 182. Recognition of the Playbook

(1) The Brotherhood recognizes the existence of the Playbook as a historical collection of social strategies, observations, and humorous traditions.

(2) The Playbook shall have no authority to override the fundamental provisions of this Code.

(3) Any strategy involving deception, pressure, harassment, or disregard for another person's autonomy shall be considered invalid.

§ 183. The Principle of Suiting Up

(1) A Brother may invoke the ceremonial principle of Suiting Up when preparing for a special occasion.

(2) Such preparation may include appropriate clothing, grooming, and confidence.

(3) Suiting Up shall not be treated as proof of superiority over other Brothers.

(4) The principle shall be understood as preparation and self-confidence, not as a guarantee of success.

§ 184. The Hot-Crazy Principle

(1) The Brotherhood recognizes the Hot-Crazy Principle as a historical humorous observation concerning attraction and compatibility.

(2) No person shall be reduced to a numerical rating or treated as an object of evaluation.

(3) Genuine compatibility, mutual respect, and emotional maturity shall take precedence over superficial comparisons.

§ 185. The Rule of the Legendary Entrance

(1) A Brother may make a memorable entrance at an appropriate gathering.

(2) Such entrance shall not involve disrupting another person's event or causing unnecessary danger.

(3) The Council may recognize particularly memorable entrances in the Brotherhood Archive.

§ 186. The Rule of the Perfect Excuse

(1) A Brother shall not be required to invent elaborate explanations for declining an invitation.

(2) A simple and honest refusal shall ordinarily be sufficient.

(3) No Brother shall demand personal details that are not reasonably necessary.

§ 187. The Rule of the Unexpected Reunion

(1) Brothers unexpectedly reunited after a significant period of separation may invoke the Reunion Principle.

(2) Such reunion shall be recognized as an opportunity to renew friendship.

(3) The passage of time shall not automatically invalidate a genuine Brotherhood bond.

§ 188. Historical Precedents

(1) Previous decisions of the Council may serve as interpretative precedents.

(2) Precedents shall not override the express wording of this Code.

(3) The Council may depart from an earlier precedent where fairness or changed circumstances require it.

§ 189. The Rule of Legendary Status

(1) A Brother may be recognized as Legendary for extraordinary contributions to the Brotherhood.

(2) Such recognition may be granted for:

a) exceptional loyalty;

b) remarkable acts of assistance;

c) memorable achievements;

d) outstanding leadership;

e) long-term dedication.

(3) Legendary status shall be honorary and shall not grant immunity from the Code.

TITLE XXV — COMMUNICATION, LANGUAGE AND THE SACRED ART OF CONVERSATION

§ 190. The Duty of Clear Communication

(1) Every Brother shall make a reasonable effort to communicate his intentions clearly.

(2) Deliberate ambiguity shall not be used to manipulate another Brother.

(3) Important arrangements shall be confirmed whenever reasonably necessary.

§ 191. The Rule of the Group Chat Announcement

(1) Information concerning the entire Brotherhood should be communicated through an appropriate shared channel.

(2) No Brother shall deliberately withhold essential information from selected members.

(3) Repeated announcements concerning matters already resolved may be considered unnecessary administrative activity.

§ 192. The Voice Message Principle

(1) Voice messages shall be permitted as a form of communication.

(2) A Brother sending an unnecessarily long voice message shall accept that the recipient may request a summary.

(3) No Brother shall be obligated to listen to a forty-minute explanation of a story that could reasonably have been told in five.

§ 193. The Rule of the Unanswered Call

(1) A missed call shall not automatically constitute disrespect.

(2) A Brother receiving a missed call should respond when reasonably available.

(3) Repeated calls may be justified in genuine emergencies.

(4) Calling seven times to ask what someone wants for dinner shall not constitute an emergency.

§ 194. The Rule of Context

(1) Statements shall be interpreted with reasonable consideration for their context.

(2) No Brother shall deliberately remove relevant context to misrepresent another Brother's words.

(3) Sarcasm and humor shall not automatically excuse genuinely harmful conduct.

§ 195. The Right to Silence

(1) Every Brother shall retain the right not to participate in a conversation.

(2) Silence shall not automatically be interpreted as hostility.

(3) No Brother shall be forced to disclose personal matters merely to satisfy curiosity.

§ 196. The Rule of the Inside Joke

(1) Inside jokes may be recognized as an informal tradition of the Brotherhood.

(2) Such jokes shall not be used to systematically exclude or humiliate a Brother.

(3) The historical origin of an inside joke may be preserved in the Brotherhood Archive.

§ 197. The Rule of Nicknames

(1) A Brother may be assigned an honorary nickname through mutual acceptance.

(2) No nickname shall be used to deliberately degrade another Brother.

(3) A Brother shall retain the right to reject a nickname that causes genuine discomfort.

§ 198. The Sacred Phrase

(1) Certain expressions may acquire special meaning through repeated use within the Brotherhood.

(2) Such expressions may be recorded in the Official Dictionary of Brotherhood Language.

(3) No expression shall possess legal authority unless expressly established by this Code.

TITLE XXVI — TRANSPORTATION, VEHICLES AND ROADSIDE BROTHERHOOD

§ 199. The Driver's Authority

(1) The driver of a vehicle shall retain final authority over its safe operation.

(2) Passengers shall comply with reasonable safety instructions.

(3) No Brother shall distract the driver deliberately.

§ 200. The Front Passenger Seat

(1) The front passenger seat may be subject to an informal priority system established among the Brothers.

(2) The driver shall retain the right to determine seating arrangements.

(3) No Brother shall create a dangerous argument over the front passenger seat.

§ 201. The Rule of Navigation

(1) A Brother assigned as navigator shall make reasonable efforts to assist the driver.

(2) Navigation shall include providing accurate directions where available.

(3) Deliberately giving incorrect directions as a prank may constitute misconduct where it creates significant inconvenience or danger.

§ 202. The Rule of the Music Selection

(1) Music selection during a shared journey may be decided by agreement.

(2) The driver may adjust or stop music whenever necessary for safe driving.

(3) No Brother shall claim permanent ownership of the vehicle's audio system merely because he connected first.

§ 203. The Rule of the Fuel Contribution

(1) Where passengers agree to contribute towards fuel costs, the agreed contributions shall be respected.

(2) Contributions shall be communicated clearly.

(3) No passenger shall be charged an unreasonable amount without prior agreement.

§ 204. The Rule of Vehicle Cleanliness

(1) Every Brother shall respect the cleanliness of a vehicle belonging to another Brother.

(2) Litter shall not be deliberately left behind.

(3) Any significant damage or mess shall be acknowledged and reasonably addressed.

§ 205. The Roadside Assistance Principle

(1) A Brother encountering another Brother in roadside difficulty should make reasonable efforts to assist.

(2) Assistance may include contacting appropriate services, arranging transportation, or providing other safe support.

(3) No Brother shall be expected to perform dangerous roadside repairs beyond his competence.

§ 206. The Rule of the Road Trip

(1) A Road Trip shall be treated as a shared undertaking.

(2) Participants shall agree upon major destinations, expected costs, and reasonable travel arrangements.

(3) Changes to the itinerary shall be communicated to the group.

(4) No Brother shall be forced to continue a journey against his reasonable wishes.

§ 207. The Rule of Safe Arrival

(1) Brothers travelling separately to or from a shared event may agree to confirm their safe arrival.

(2) Such confirmation shall be encouraged where circumstances justify it.

(3) Failure to send a message shall not automatically establish wrongdoing.

TITLE XXVII — CHALLENGES, COMPETITIONS AND GAMES OF HONOR

§ 208. Recognition of Brotherhood Challenges

(1) A Brotherhood Challenge shall mean a voluntary competition between two or more Brothers.

(2) Challenges may involve skill, knowledge, creativity, sports, gaming, or other harmless activities.

(3) Every challenge shall respect the dignity and safety of its participants.

§ 209. The Right to Challenge

(1) Any Brother may propose a reasonable challenge.

(2) The challenged Brother shall retain the right to decline.

(3) Refusal shall not automatically constitute cowardice or disloyalty.

§ 210. The Rule of Fair Competition

(1) Every competition shall be governed by rules established before it begins.

(2) No Brother shall secretly alter the rules after the result becomes apparent.

(3) Participants shall accept legitimate outcomes.

§ 211. The Rule of the Rematch

(1) A Brother who loses a competition may request a rematch.

(2) The opposing Brother may accept or decline.

(3) A rematch shall not invalidate the original result.

(4) The phrase "best of three" shall only be binding where agreed before the competition.

§ 212. The Rule of the Controller

(1) Where gaming equipment is shared, all participants shall receive reasonable access.

(2) Equipment settings shall not be deliberately altered to disadvantage another participant.

(3) A Brother shall not be blamed for a malfunctioning controller without reasonable evidence.

§ 213. The Rule of the Excuse

(1) A Brother may explain the circumstances of an unexpected loss.

(2) Such explanation shall not alter the official result.

(3) Repeated references to lag, controller drift, or unfortunate matchmaking shall not automatically justify a rematch.

§ 214. The Rule of the Victory

(1) A winning Brother may celebrate his achievement.

(2) Celebration shall remain proportionate and respectful.

(3) Victory shall not grant authority to humiliate the defeated Brother.

§ 215. The Rule of the Defeat

(1) A defeated Brother shall be treated with dignity.

(2) Losing shall not justify insults or deliberate sabotage.

(3) A Brother who accepts defeat gracefully shall be entitled to recognition.

§ 216. The Championship Record

(1) Significant Brotherhood competitions may be recorded in an official Championship Register.

(2) The Register may include:

a) the participants;

b) the competition;

c) the final result;

d) the date;

e) the name of the champion.

(3) Records shall be maintained honestly.

TITLE XXVIII — THE OFFICIAL CALENDAR AND ANNUAL BROTHERHOOD EVENTS

§ 217. Establishment of the Brotherhood Calendar

(1) The Brotherhood may maintain an Official Calendar of significant events.

(2) The Calendar shall serve as a reference for gatherings, anniversaries, competitions, and traditional celebrations.

(3) Inclusion in the Calendar shall not create an obligation to attend.

§ 218. The Founding Day

(1) The anniversary of the Brotherhood's establishment may be recognized as Founding Day.

(2) Founding Day shall commemorate the creation and continued existence of the Brotherhood.

(3) The occasion may include a gathering, review of historical events, and recognition of significant contributions.

§ 219. The Annual Council Assembly

(1) An Annual Council Assembly may be held once per calendar year.

(2) The Assembly shall review:

a) the condition of the Brotherhood;

b) significant disputes and resolutions;

c) proposed amendments;

d) upcoming events;

e) outstanding responsibilities.

(3) A summary of the Assembly may be preserved in the official records.

§ 220. The Brotherhood Anniversary

(1) The Brotherhood may celebrate the anniversary of its founding or another historically significant date.

(2) The celebration shall be organized according to the available resources and preferences of participating Brothers.

(3) No Brother shall be required to spend money beyond his means.

§ 221. The Day of the Wingman

(1) The Brotherhood may designate one annual day to recognize the contributions of reliable Wingmen.

(2) Recognition may be granted for loyalty, encouragement, and exceptional assistance.

(3) The award shall not depend upon romantic success.

§ 222. The Day of Reconciliation

(1) The Brotherhood may designate an annual occasion for resolving outstanding disagreements.

(2) Participation shall be voluntary.

(3) No Brother shall be forced to discuss a personal matter publicly.

§ 223. The Memorial of Former Days

(1) The Brotherhood may preserve memories of former members, important gatherings, and significant historical events.

(2) Historical records shall be maintained respectfully.

(3) No Brother shall be required to participate in commemorations.

§ 224. The Closing of the Brotherhood Year

(1) At the conclusion of each Brotherhood year, the Council may issue an annual summary.

(2) The summary may recognize achievements, memorable events, and proposed improvements.

(3) The purpose of the annual summary shall be reflection rather than judgment.

TITLE XXIX — HEALTH, WELL-BEING AND MUTUAL PROTECTION

§ 225. The Duty of Mutual Care

(1) Every Brother shall show reasonable concern for the physical and emotional well-being of fellow Brothers.

(2) A Brother experiencing difficulties shall not be treated as a burden.

(3) Assistance shall be offered with respect for the affected Brother's independence.

§ 226. The Right to Ask for Help

(1) Every Brother shall retain the right to request assistance without fear of ridicule.

(2) Asking for help shall not constitute weakness or failure.

(3) No Brother shall deliberately use another Brother's request for help as material for humiliation.

§ 227. The Rule of the Unwell Brother

(1) Where a Brother becomes ill during a gathering or shared activity, reasonable efforts shall be made to assist him.

(2) The activity may be postponed or ended where necessary.

(3) No Brother shall be pressured to continue an activity against reasonable health advice.

§ 228. Emotional Support

(1) Brothers shall make reasonable efforts to listen when another Brother wishes to discuss a personal difficulty.

(2) Immediate solutions shall not always be required.

(3) A Brother may offer advice, but shall not assume that advice is always desired.

§ 229. The Right to Professional Assistance

(1) Every Brother shall retain the right to seek assistance from qualified professionals.

(2) No Brother shall discourage another from obtaining appropriate medical or psychological support.

(3) Personal difficulties shall not automatically become public Brotherhood matters.

§ 230. The Rule of Rest

(1) Rest shall be recognized as a legitimate personal need.

(2) A Brother shall not be considered disloyal for declining an activity due to exhaustion.

(3) No Brotherhood tradition shall require a Brother to disregard reasonable limits.

§ 231. The Emergency Assistance Principle

(1) Where a Brother faces an immediate threat to his safety, appropriate assistance shall take priority over ordinary Brotherhood activities.

(2) Brothers shall contact emergency services where necessary.

(3) No Brother shall be expected to place himself in unreasonable danger while attempting to help.

§ 232. Protection Against Peer Pressure

(1) No Brother shall pressure another Brother into consuming substances, taking unnecessary risks, or participating in harmful activities.

(2) Refusal shall be respected without argument.

(3) The Brotherhood shall not confuse reckless behavior with courage.

§ 233. The Rule of Safe Transportation

(1) Every Brother shall support safe transportation arrangements.

(2) No Brother shall operate a vehicle while impaired, dangerously tired, or otherwise unfit to drive.

(3) A Brother requiring a safe way home shall receive reasonable assistance where possible.

TITLE XXX — THE RIGHTS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF NEW BROTHERS

§ 234. Recognition of New Brothers

(1) A new Brother shall be a person voluntarily welcomed into the Brotherhood.

(2) Membership shall be based upon mutual respect, trust, and friendship.

(3) No initiation shall require humiliation, coercion, dangerous conduct, or the violation of personal boundaries.

§ 235. The Welcome Principle

(1) Existing Brothers shall make reasonable efforts to welcome new members.

(2) New Brothers shall receive an explanation of the principal traditions and rules.

(3) No new Brother shall be expected to know every provision of this Code immediately.

§ 236. The Probationary Period

(1) The Brotherhood may establish a voluntary introductory period for new members.

(2) Such period shall serve to establish familiarity and mutual trust.

(3) It shall not be used to create an inferior class of members.

(4) Upon completion, the new Brother may receive full recognition.

§ 237. Rights of New Brothers

A new Brother shall possess the right to:

a) be treated with dignity;

b) express opinions;

c) decline unreasonable requests;

d) ask questions concerning the Code;

e) maintain personal privacy;

f) participate in Brotherhood activities voluntarily.

§ 238. Responsibilities of New Brothers

(1) New Brothers shall make reasonable efforts to respect established traditions.

(2) They shall communicate honestly with other members.

(3) They shall respect the personal property and boundaries of fellow Brothers.

(4) They shall be subject to the same fundamental standards as all other members.

§ 239. Prohibition of Initiation Abuse

(1) No Brother shall use initiation as an excuse for bullying or exploitation.

(2) No initiation shall involve physical danger, illegal conduct, forced consumption, or public humiliation.

(3) Any violation of this provision may constitute a serious offence.

§ 240. Equal Status After Admission

(1) Upon receiving full membership, a Brother shall possess equal fundamental status.

(2) Previous membership duration shall not justify permanent discrimination.

(3) Every Brother shall have the opportunity to contribute to the Brotherhood.

TITLE XXXI — BROTHERHOOD RECORDS, ARCHIVES AND HISTORICAL DOCUMENTS

§ 241. Establishment of the Official Archive

(1) The Brotherhood may maintain an Official Archive.

(2) The Archive shall preserve significant documents and historical records.

(3) Its purpose shall be to maintain continuity between past, present, and future members.

§ 242. Official Documents

The Official Archive may contain:

a) editions of the Bro Code;

b) approved amendments;

c) Council decisions;

d) records of significant events;

e) membership records;

f) honorary awards;

g) historical photographs.

§ 243. The Keeper of the Archive

(1) The Council may appoint an Archivist.

(2) The Archivist shall maintain the accuracy and organization of the Archive.

(3) The Archivist shall not alter official documents without authorization.

(4) Corrections shall be recorded transparently.

§ 244. Preservation of Original Editions

(1) Every official edition of the Code shall be preserved where reasonably possible.

(2) Amendments shall not result in the unauthorized destruction of earlier editions.

(3) Historical versions shall be clearly distinguished from the currently effective edition.

§ 245. The Historical Record

(1) Significant Brotherhood events may be recorded for historical purposes.

(2) Records shall distinguish confirmed facts from personal recollections.

(3) No Brother shall deliberately falsify historical records to obtain recognition or avoid responsibility.

§ 246. The Right to Inspect Records

(1) Recognized Brothers may request access to non-confidential Brotherhood records.

(2) Personal information and confidential proceedings shall remain protected.

(3) Access may be restricted where disclosure would violate another person's reasonable privacy.

§ 247. The Official Seal

(1) The Brotherhood may establish an Official Seal.

(2) The Seal may be used to authenticate approved editions, certificates, and Council decisions.

(3) Unauthorized use of the Seal to create false official documents shall constitute misconduct.

§ 248. The Rule of Historical Accuracy

(1) Historical records shall not be rewritten merely to make a Brother appear more heroic.

(2) Legendary stories may contain humorous exaggeration, provided they are clearly distinguished from official historical records.

(3) The Archive shall preserve both the achievements and lessons of the Brotherhood.

TITLE XXXII — INTERPRETATION, ENFORCEMENT AND FINAL LEGAL PRINCIPLES

§ 249. Official Interpretation

(1) The Council shall serve as the principal authority responsible for interpreting this Code.

(2) Interpretation shall consider the wording, purpose, and fundamental principles of each provision.

(3) No interpretation shall contradict an express fundamental right.

§ 250. Consistency of Enforcement

(1) Similar violations shall ordinarily receive similar treatment.

(2) Differences in circumstances may justify different outcomes.

(3) Personal friendships shall not determine the application of disciplinary rules.

§ 251. Prohibition of Arbitrary Enforcement

(1) No Brother shall be punished solely because another Brother dislikes him.

(2) Every disciplinary decision shall have a reasonable factual basis.

(3) The burden of establishing a violation shall rest upon the party making the accusation.

§ 252. The Principle of Good Faith

(1) Every provision of this Code shall be applied in good faith.

(2) No Brother shall exploit technical wording to justify conduct that clearly violates the fundamental principles of Brotherhood.

(3) No Brother shall invent obligations that do not reasonably arise from this Code.

§ 253. Severability

(1) Where any provision of this Code is found invalid or unenforceable within the Brotherhood, the remaining provisions shall continue to apply.

(2) The Council shall consider whether the affected provision should be amended or replaced.

§ 254. Non-Retroactivity

(1) No Brother shall be disciplined under a provision adopted after the conduct in question.

(2) Amendments shall ordinarily apply from their effective date.

(3) A later amendment may clarify an existing rule but shall not be used unfairly to impose a new punishment for past conduct.

§ 255. Supremacy of Fundamental Rights

(1) The fundamental rights established by this Code shall prevail over conflicting informal traditions.

(2) No Council decision shall eliminate the right to dignity, fair treatment, personal autonomy, or reasonable privacy.

(3) Any decision violating this provision shall be subject to appeal.

§ 256. The Ultimate Interpretation

(1) Where two reasonable interpretations of a provision exist, preference shall be given to the interpretation that best preserves trust and fairness.

(2) Loyalty shall never be interpreted as ownership, obedience, or unconditional agreement.

(3) Brotherhood shall remain a voluntary relationship founded upon mutual respect.

TITLE XXXIII — AWARDS, HONORS AND DECORATIONS

§ 257. Establishment of Brotherhood Honors

(1) The Brotherhood may establish official honors to recognize exceptional contributions.

(2) Honors shall be awarded for demonstrated conduct rather than popularity or personal influence.

(3) No Brother shall be entitled to an award merely because he requests one.

§ 258. The Order of the Golden Bro

(1) The Order of the Golden Bro shall constitute the highest ordinary honorary distinction of the Brotherhood.

(2) It may be awarded for extraordinary loyalty, courage, reliability, or long-term dedication.

(3) The award shall require approval by a two-thirds majority of the Council.

§ 259. The Medal of Loyalty

(1) The Medal of Loyalty may be awarded to a Brother who has consistently demonstrated exceptional trustworthiness.

(2) Particular consideration shall be given to Brothers who provide assistance without expecting personal reward.

(3) The Medal may be awarded posthumously.

§ 260. The Order of the Wingman

(1) The Order of the Wingman may be awarded for outstanding social support and consideration.

(2) Romantic success shall not be a condition of eligibility.

(3) The recipient shall be recognized for honesty, encouragement, and respect for personal boundaries.

§ 261. The Medal of Legendary Patience

(1) The Medal of Legendary Patience may be awarded to a Brother who has demonstrated exceptional tolerance during prolonged Brotherhood difficulties.

(2) Such difficulties may include:

a) repeated changes of plans;

b) excessively long decision-making;

c) technical failures during important gaming sessions;

d) the inability of the group to agree upon a restaurant.

(3) The award may be granted humorously but shall not be used to ridicule its recipient.

§ 262. The Order of the Unsung Hero

(1) This distinction shall recognize Brothers whose contributions are significant but frequently overlooked.

(2) Examples may include organizing events, assisting members, maintaining equipment, and resolving logistical problems.

(3) Recognition shall not depend upon public visibility.

§ 263. The Lifetime Brotherhood Award

(1) The Lifetime Brotherhood Award may be granted to a Brother whose dedication has extended over a significant period.

(2) It shall recognize the lasting positive influence of the recipient.

(3) The award shall not grant immunity from future disciplinary proceedings.

§ 264. Revocation of Honors

(1) An honorary distinction may be revoked where it was obtained through deliberate deception.

(2) Revocation shall require a formal Council decision.

(3) A distinction shall not be revoked merely because of a personal disagreement.

TITLE XXXIV — BROTHERHOOD OATHS AND CEREMONIES

§ 265. Recognition of the Brotherhood Oath

(1) The Brotherhood may establish an official oath expressing its fundamental principles.

(2) The oath shall be voluntary.

(3) No Brother shall be required to make promises that conflict with his personal integrity or safety.

§ 266. The Founding Oath

Every founding member may voluntarily declare:

"I stand among my Brothers by choice, not by obligation. I shall act with honesty, protect trust, respect every Brother's dignity, and never confuse loyalty with blind obedience. I shall uphold the Code and remember that the Brotherhood is only as strong as the respect shared by its members."

§ 267. The Admission Ceremony

(1) A new Brother may be formally welcomed through an admission ceremony.

(2) The ceremony may include:

a) an introduction to the Brotherhood;

b) a reading of the fundamental principles;

c) voluntary acceptance of the Brotherhood Oath;

d) recognition by existing members.

(3) No dangerous or humiliating initiation shall be permitted.

§ 268. The Renewal of the Oath

(1) The Brotherhood may hold an annual ceremony during which members voluntarily reaffirm their commitment.

(2) Renewal shall serve as a reminder of the principles of the Code.

(3) A Brother shall not lose membership solely because he declines to participate in the ceremony.

§ 269. The Ceremony of Reconciliation

(1) Where a Brother has restored trust following a serious dispute, the Brotherhood may recognize the reconciliation.

(2) Such recognition shall require the voluntary agreement of the persons directly involved.

(3) The ceremony shall not erase the past or force forgiveness.

§ 270. The Ceremony of Recognition

(1) Honors and distinctions may be presented during an official Brotherhood gathering.

(2) The reason for each award shall be announced.

(3) The recipient may decline public recognition.

§ 271. The Rule of the Official Toast

(1) An official Brotherhood toast may accompany significant ceremonies.

(2) Participation shall remain voluntary.

(3) Any suitable non-alcoholic beverage may be used.

(4) The words of the toast shall reflect unity, respect, and friendship.

§ 272. The Final Ceremonial Declaration

The following declaration may be used at the conclusion of an official gathering:

"Different paths, different stories, one Brotherhood. We stand together not because we must, but because we choose to."

TITLE XXXV — THE SUPREME COUNCIL AND CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY

§ 273. Establishment of the Supreme Council

(1) The Supreme Council shall constitute the highest constitutional authority within the Brotherhood.

(2) It shall be responsible for preserving the integrity of this Code.

(3) The Supreme Council shall operate according to the principles of fairness, transparency, and collective responsibility.

§ 274. Composition of the Supreme Council

(1) The Supreme Council shall consist of designated representatives of the Brotherhood.

(2) Its composition shall be determined through an approved constitutional procedure.

(3) No member shall possess unlimited authority.

§ 275. Powers of the Supreme Council

The Supreme Council may:

a) approve constitutional amendments;

b) review extraordinary disciplinary decisions;

c) resolve disputes concerning the interpretation of the Constitution;

d) recognize official Brotherhood institutions;

e) establish permanent honorary distinctions;

f) approve major changes to the structure of the Brotherhood.

§ 276. Limitations of Supreme Authority

(1) The Supreme Council shall remain subject to the fundamental principles of this Code.

(2) No decision shall authorize coercion, humiliation, exploitation, or deliberate harm.

(3) The Supreme Council shall not claim authority over the private lives of members beyond matters reasonably connected to the Brotherhood.

§ 277. Constitutional Review

(1) The Supreme Council may review any provision of this Code.

(2) Such review may be initiated by a formal proposal or by a demonstrated need for clarification.

(3) Proposed revisions shall be communicated to the membership.

§ 278. Extraordinary Decisions

(1) Extraordinary decisions shall require a clearly documented justification.

(2) Such decisions shall be limited to the circumstances that necessitated them.

(3) The Council shall not use extraordinary authority to avoid ordinary voting procedures.

§ 279. Removal of Council Members

(1) A Council member may be removed for serious abuse of authority, persistent misconduct, or inability to perform his responsibilities.

(2) Removal shall require a fair review.

(3) The affected member shall receive an opportunity to respond.

§ 280. Continuity of Authority

(1) The temporary absence of Council members shall not invalidate the entire legal framework of the Brotherhood.

(2) Temporary replacements may be appointed according to established procedures.

(3) The transfer of authority shall be documented.

TITLE XXXVI — DISSOLUTION, REFORMATION AND LEGACY

§ 281. Voluntary Dissolution

(1) The Brotherhood may voluntarily dissolve where its members collectively determine that continuation is no longer desired.

(2) Dissolution shall require a formal decision under the constitutional voting procedure.

(3) No Brother shall be forced to remain a member.

§ 282. Suspension of Brotherhood Activities

(1) The Brotherhood may temporarily suspend its organized activities.

(2) Suspension shall not automatically constitute dissolution.

(3) Existing financial obligations and property arrangements shall remain subject to their original agreements.

§ 283. Reformation of the Brotherhood

(1) A dissolved or inactive Brotherhood may be re-established through a voluntary founding agreement.

(2) Previous traditions and historical records may be preserved.

(3) Reformation shall not automatically restore former leadership positions.

§ 284. Preservation of the Code

(1) The official editions of this Code may be preserved as historical documents.

(2) A dissolved Brotherhood may retain its historical identity through its records and traditions.

(3) Historical preservation shall not create obligations for former members.

§ 285. The Legacy of a Brother

(1) The positive contributions of a Brother may be preserved in the historical record.

(2) Legacy shall be based upon actions, character, and lasting influence.

(3) No Brother shall be remembered solely for his mistakes where he has demonstrated genuine growth.

§ 286. The Legacy of the Founders

(1) The Founding Brothers shall be remembered for establishing the original principles of the Brotherhood.

(2) Their decisions may serve as historical guidance.

(3) Their authority shall not extend beyond the limits established by this Code.

§ 287. The Survival of Brotherhood

(1) The Brotherhood shall not depend solely upon a particular location, gathering place, or communication platform.

(2) Members may remain Brothers despite distance, changing circumstances, or extended periods without contact.

(3) The continuation of Brotherhood shall depend upon mutual respect and voluntary commitment.

§ 288. The Last Article

(1) No written Code can guarantee loyalty.

(2) No Council can manufacture friendship.

(3) No ceremony can replace genuine trust.

(4) The provisions of this Book shall therefore be understood as guidance for conduct, not as a substitute for character.
